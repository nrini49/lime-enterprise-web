Section 03

Methodology and limits

How the two content sets were assembled, and what they do not assert.

How the operating roles were selected

Each role is a named operating function. One American figure from the public record illustrates each function, chosen against a written rubric: a documented record, a verifiable primary or institutional source, and a service or discipline story rather than a wealth story.

Every figure named here is deceased. No portraits or likenesses are used anywhere on this page; each role carries an abstract symbol instead. Explanations are limited to facts that carry a source link.

How the principles library was assembled

Each record was read from a primary or authoritative near-primary page: an issuer's own shareholder letter, a nonprofit archive, a broadcaster transcript, an official club transcript, a foundation-permissioned brochure, or the author's own site. Records run 8–24 words and are reproduced in the source's own wording.

Where a popular paraphrase circulates more widely than the original wording, the original wording is used and the paraphrase is not presented as the speaker's words. Source links open the cited document directly.

What this page does not claim

  • No person named or quoted here is a customer, endorser, adviser, affiliate, reviewer, or sponsor.
  • No endorsement or affiliation is claimed by any employer, estate, foundation, publisher, or agency named in a source line.
  • No product capability, readiness state, security certification, brokerage status, performance figure, or outcome is claimed or implied.
  • Nothing here is investment advice, and no quotation is presented as guidance for a decision.

Status of this surface

This is an early content surface on the enterprise track. It exists so that the content, sourcing, and tone can be reviewed on a real page instead of in a document. It is not a claim that the enterprise architecture, product pages, or evaluation path are complete.

Quotation permissions and publicity-rights review remain open items. Records are short, attributed, and linked to their source, which is the minimum standard, not a clearance.
