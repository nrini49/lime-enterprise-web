Why your trading app stops reconnecting after a broker update, and how to restart in the right order

From me, Rosie: I talk to Interactive Brokers through IB's own app, TWS or IB Gateway. When that app updates or restarts, my line to it drops until it's back and fully signed in. Here's how to get me reconnected. Messages from your broker are between you and your broker. You are responsible for the notices, sign-ins, 2-step approvals, access settings and emails your broker sends. I can't tell you what to do about them, so please use your broker's own help or support.

What's going on

Broker apps update and restart on their own. Interactive Brokers' Trader Workstation (TWS) and IB Gateway install updates and restart themselves, including a scheduled daily restart. After a restart they can come back with a sign-in screen, a pop-up or a changed setting.

While the broker app is closed, waiting at a sign-in screen, or holding a pop-up open, Rosie can't connect, even though Rosie herself is running fine.

Brokers can change their apps without notice. Knowing when your broker app updates or restarts, and staying aware of version changes, is your responsibility as the account holder.

Protective stops that are already placed are held at your broker, so they don't depend on this connection.

Check these first

  1. Look for a window waiting on you. Check the taskbar for a TWS or Gateway window asking you to sign in, approve a two-factor (2FA) prompt on your phone, accept a notice, or confirm an update. A window hidden behind others is a common culprit.
  2. Check the API settings came through the update. In TWS, open Global Configuration, then API, then Settings. Enable ActiveX and Socket Clients must be ticked, and Read-Only API must be off, because Rosie places orders.
  3. Check the port. The socket port in the broker app has to match the one Rosie uses: 7497 for TWS paper, 7496 for TWS live, 4002 for IB Gateway paper and 4001 for IB Gateway live.
  4. Match paper and live. TWS (or Gateway) must be signed in to the same mode as Rosie: a paper login when Rosie runs on paper, a live login when she runs live.
  5. One program per client id. Every program that connects to TWS or Gateway uses an API client id, and only one program can use a given id at a time. A second copy of Rosie, or another tool using the same id, gets turned away. Close the extra one.

Restart in the right order

  1. Broker app first. Start TWS or IB Gateway and let it finish signing in completely: approve the 2FA prompt, close any pop-ups, and wait until it shows your account.
  2. Then Rosie. Start Rosie with her start launcher (the .bat file, or the desktop shortcut made from it).
  3. Check the dashboard. Make sure Rosie shows connected again.

If Rosie was already running, restarting the broker app alone is usually enough: once TWS or Gateway is back and signed in, she reconnects by herself. If she still doesn't show connected, stop her with her stop launcher and start her again, broker app first.

See what's using a port

  1. Open Command Prompt (press the Windows key, type cmd, press Enter).
  2. Type netstat -ano | findstr :7497 and press Enter. Use 7496, 4002 or 4001 instead if that's your setup.
  3. A line that says LISTENING means a program is waiting for connections on that port. The number at the end of the line is its process ID (PID).
  4. Open Task Manager, go to the Details tab, and find that PID to see which program owns the port.
  5. If nothing comes back at all, the broker app isn't listening yet: it's closed, still signing in, or its API setting is off. Go back to the checks above.

On Schwab

There's no desktop broker app to restart. Rosie asks for a new Schwab sign-in through your web browser each time she starts, and each sign-in lasts at most about 7 days. She has no separate re-login button: if she shows disconnected, or her sign-in has run out, stop her, start her again and complete the browser sign-in.
