Your life with Rosie

From Rosie

I monitor your trades. You enjoy your day.

Six small pleasures, in plain words. Not one of them is a number. Numbers are my department; your day is yours.

  • Your hours, returned.

    No checking at lunch, in the car, or at 3 a.m. Watching is my job, and I adore it, so your attention can go to the people and things you love.

  • A quieter mind.

    Every position I open arrives with a protective stop placed at your broker, from the very first moment. So “what if something happens while I’m away?” already has an answer, written before you thought to ask.

    Panic or boom, I follow the same written rules. Fear and excitement may request an audience; they never get to make the calls.

  • A well-run house.

    I keep the risk small, written down, and checked before every order. Your account is run like a house with standards, never like a night at the tables.

I do the watching. You do the living. Everyone gets the part they do best.

Rosie's rules

From Rosie

All my rules, in plain words

These are the guardrails I follow on every order, with Schwab and with Interactive Brokers.

  1. A stop on every buy. Every buy is a limit order, never a market order, and it goes in with one protective stop held at your broker as a good-till-cancelled order, so the stop stays in place whether you’re watching or not.
  2. A cap on every buy. Conservative: up to 2 shares and $500. Normal: up to 5 shares and $1,250. Aggressive: up to 10 shares and $2,500. You pick the level, and my automatic buys always use Conservative.
  3. A daily loss limit. If your account falls 3% from the day’s start, I make no new buys until the next trading day.
  4. A quiet open. No new buys in the first 15 minutes after the 9:30 a.m. ET open.
  5. A daily cap on automatic trades. At most 20 automatic orders a day, and after 3 losing automatic trades in a row, no more automatic buys that day.
  6. Your list only. I buy only symbols on my approved list.
  7. Exits are never blocked. None of these limits ever stops a sell, your stop, or closing everything.
  8. Practice first. I start in practice mode. Real-money orders only begin when you turn them on.
  9. Your keys stay home. With Schwab, your app key and sign-in stay in files on your own computer. With Interactive Brokers, you sign in to IB’s own app on your computer, and I never hold your IB password.
  10. Your account only. Orders go only to your own broker account.

Which rules you can change

Some of my rules are yours to set, some are set with you when I’m installed, and some are built in and the same for everyone.

You set these on my Workstation, any time

  • Which of your brokerage accounts I trade.
  • The risk level for each account: Conservative, Normal or Aggressive.
  • A top share price for new buys, if you want one.
  • Switching between practice and real money (once real money has been enabled at setup), and whether I may trade on my own. I start in practice, with automatic trading off.
  • STOP ALL and FLATTEN ALL, whenever you choose.

Set with you when I’m installed

These live in a settings file on your computer, not on the dashboard. Each one has a starting value and a built-in limit, and changing one means editing that file and restarting me.

  • How far below the buy price your protective stop sits: 3% to start, and never more than 7.5%.
  • Risk per trade: 2% of the account to start.
  • Total open risk: 6% of the account to start.
  • Daily loss limit: 3% to start.
  • Automatic trades: at most 20 a day and a pause after 3 losses in a row, to start.

Built in, the same for everyone

  • Every buy is a limit order with one protective stop held at your broker.
  • No stop is ever wider than 7.5%. If a stock’s ordinary overnight moves would need more room than that, I don’t buy it.
  • No new buys in the first 15 minutes after the open.
  • My automatic buys always use the Conservative cap.
  • No limit ever blocks a sell, your stop, or closing everything.

Section 08

From Rosie

I count the cost before the prize.

Before I go after any gain, I’ve already set what a single trade, or a single difficult day, is allowed to cost. Limits first, then ambition.

  • A protective stop on every position

    Every position I hold has a fixed protective stop, and it lives at your broker, so it keeps working even with your computer switched off.

  • Conservative, Normal or Aggressive

    Each buy is capped by the choice you set: Conservative 2 shares and $500, Normal 5 shares and $1,250, Aggressive 10 shares and $2,500. Automatic buys always use the Conservative cap.

  • 2% risk per trade

    I also size each position so that, at its stop, it risks no more than 2% of the account. If the per-buy cap is tighter, the cap wins.

  • 6% total open-risk cap

    Add up the risk on everything I have open, and I won’t let it pass 6%. I’m gracious about many things; that isn’t one of them.

  • Daily loss limit and kill switch

    When the day’s loss limit is reached, I stop opening new trades for the day. No chasing.

  • STOP ALL and FLATTEN ALL

    STOP ALL turns me off and cancels pending buys, and leaves the protective stops on positions you already hold. FLATTEN ALL is separate: it closes every open position, and you type a confirmation first. Both are always yours.

  • Practice first, so you can judge me

    In your free one-month evaluation I run in practice mode, with no live capital and no real-money orders, before a single real dollar is involved.

  • I refuse a symbol without measured price history; I never give it a guessed stop.
  • I run on your own computer. Your broker login stays with your broker.
  • Limits reduce how much a trade or a day can cost. They do not eliminate losses.

Rosie · Evidence

From Rosie

Rules I keep. Receipts I show.

During your free evaluation I run in practice mode only, and I don't publish profit figures; I'd rather show you my rules than flatter you with numbers. Here is what every order of mine has to pass, how it's checked, and what I won't do.

A beauty in digits: the rules every order has to pass

Protective stop

3% below entry

I place a protective stop at your broker on every position. I send it together with the buy, for the same number of shares. It starts 3% below the entry price and can never be more than 7.5% below. With Interactive Brokers I may give a stock a little more room when its ordinary overnight moves are bigger, still within 7.5%. If a stock would need more room than that, I don’t buy it. If you sell part of a position, I put the stop back on the shares you still hold, at the same price.

Source: my written protection rules, on both Schwab and Interactive Brokers

Per-buy caps

2 / $500

You choose Conservative, Normal or Aggressive. Conservative allows up to 2 shares and $500 per buy; Normal 5 shares and $1,250; Aggressive 10 shares and $2,500. Automatic buys, if you turn them on, always use the Conservative cap. These sit on top of the 2% and 6% limits; whichever is tighter wins.

Source: my written risk-choice rules, on both Schwab and Interactive Brokers

Risk per trade

2% of equity

I also size every position so that, if its stop is reached, the loss at the stop price is no more than 2% of the account. A fast market can still fill below the stop; I won't pretend otherwise.

Source: my written sizing rules

Total open risk

6% of equity

Every order I send passes the same safety checks first. Everything I have open, plus anything pending, may put no more than 6% of the account at risk. An order that would go over is refused, politely and absolutely.

Source: my written risk limits

Trailing stops

Off

A trailing stop follows the price up and sells when it turns back. I don’t use them: your protective stop does not follow the price. A position closes when you approve one of my exit proposals, when I sell it on my own, which I do only if you have turned on automatic trading, when you close it yourself, or when the stop is reached.

Source: my written protection rules, checked by an automated test

Automated tests

Checked

My rules are checked by an automated test suite before a change is shipped. I don't print a dated scoreboard here, because a stale date would be a poor receipt.

Five TradingView indicators, from the house that built me

Lime Signalworks also makes its own TradingView indicators: tools that turn a chart into plain readings you can check, instead of a guess. They work on their own, on your TradingView chart, no Rosie required (I try not to take it personally). This is the most technical corner of the page, so each one is shown with the problem it was made to solve.

  • No. 1

    Lime Color Weather

    The problem it solves You trade the same way whether the market is friendly or stormy, and only find out which it was afterwards.

    Shows the market’s current “weather” as colour bands, so you can see whether conditions favour buying, holding or staying small.

    A little technical Conditions are drawn as colour bands with readings such as “bull cooling” or “strong bull rising”. The same read appears in more than one pane, and the middle pane shows whether a tiring move is forming with or against the larger trend.

  • No. 2

    Lime Notify

    The problem it solves There are so many signals on your screen that you can’t tell what you’re actually supposed to do.

    Reduces the noise to three readings only: Buy, Trim and Exit, each checked on the 1-day, 6-hour and 3-hour time frames.

    A little technical The published reading is Alignment + Momentum − Exhaustion − Risk: Buy when it is strong, Trim when it softens, Exit when it breaks down. Tiredness and risk count against a Buy.

  • No. 3

    Lime Exhaustion

    The problem it solves You keep getting in too late, buying right as the move runs out of steam.

    Shows whether a move is fresh, mid-journey or worn out, so you can avoid buying the last gasp of a move.

    A little technical The published reading is Stretch from Trend + Momentum Extremes + Volatility Expansion − Fresh Support. The further price stretches from its trend, the more tired the move reads.

  • No. 4

    Lime StopWatcher

    The problem it solves You never know where to put your stop, so you either get shaken out or give back what you made.

    Three tools for the life of a trade: a first stop level at entry, a trailing guard as the trade moves your way, and a line that marks where a mature move may be ending.

    A little technical Three parts: StopWatcher I (First Stop), StopWatcher II (Trailing Guard) and StopWatcher III (Harvest Line), which gives its final read as CAND, AVOID, WATCH or HOLD.

  • No. 5

    Lime 8 Filter Stack

    The problem it solves When the signals are mixed, it’s easy to talk yourself into the trade anyway.

    Combines several safety checks into one pass or fail view, so you can stand aside when the signals are mixed.

  • The indicators show their logic. They support your decisions and do not make them for you.
  • Indicators do not predict the market, and no indicator removes the risk of loss.
