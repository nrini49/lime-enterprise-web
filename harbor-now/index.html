How to read Harbor Now

Harbor Now is my plain-language tour of how my trading rules read the day. I use a few sea and weather images because they are easier to picture than chart terms. Each one means exactly one thing, listed below, and each edition explains an image in brackets the first time it appears.

The three lights I line up

  1. The tide. The four main index funds (SPY, QQQ, DIA, IWM). The tide is rising when all four close above rising trend lines.
  2. The hot sector. The sector fund strongest against SPY over recent sessions and today, and whether it is rising with the tide.
  3. The strongest names inside it. Names in that sector, from my Watchlist I first, that are rising and stronger than SPY.

When all three point the same way, I say the lights line up. That is a description of the day, not a signal to trade.

The images I use, and what they mean

The harbor Trading plan and risk rules
Where I start every day: my written trading rules and protective stops. Harbor Now is my daily report from there.
The tide Market trend (broad indexes)
The direction the whole market is moving, read from the four main index funds (SPY, QQQ, DIA, IWM). The tide is rising when all four close above rising trend lines, mixed when only some do, and going out when none do.
The trend line Moving average (exponential)
A smoothed average of recent closing prices. A name is rising, in my reading, when it closes above its trend line and that line is pointing up.
The weather Volatility (VIX)
How calm or stormy price swings are. Calm means VIX, the market's fear gauge, eased. Choppy means it rose. Stormy means it jumped. The weather describes the mood of the day; it predicts nothing.
The wind picking up Momentum
Momentum: a day when the broad market moved strongly in the same direction as the tide. A wind against the tide is a strong move the other way.
The hot sector Sector relative strength leader
The one of the eleven sector funds (plus semiconductors) that has been strongest against SPY over recent sessions and today. The coolest sector is the weakest one.
A crosscurrent Divergence
The hot sector rising while the tide is not, or the other way round: the two point in different directions.
The lights line up Signal convergence
Sailors enter a harbor by lining up two lights on shore. My three lights are the tide rising, the hot sector rising with it, and the strongest names inside that sector rising too. When all three point the same way, I say they line up.
A headwind Resistance
A price level above the market where earlier rises ran out of steam. Prices meeting a headwind often slow down; sometimes they push through.
An anchorage Support
A price level below the market where earlier declines came to rest. It describes the past; it is no promise the next decline stops there.

How to use Harbor Now well

Harbor Now is my dated, after-the-close read of the market. It describes conditions; it recommends nothing. It is a lighthouse, not a steering wheel. These six habits make it read best.

  1. The date comes first. Every edition is stamped with the close it describes. Last Tuesday's report is history, not news. When the newest edition is more than a few days old, the page says so right at the top.
  2. Mood before numbers. The headline and its one-line summary give the shape of the day in a single breath: broad green, broad red, mixed, calm or jumpy. I take in the mood before any single figure.
  3. Four funds, side by side. SPY, QQQ, DIA and IWM together show whether a move was broad or narrow. One green fund among four red ones is a narrow day, and the report says so plainly.
  4. Volatility is the room temperature. VIX rising or easing shows how nervous the market felt. It colours the day; it predicts nothing.
  5. A week tells a story. One edition is a snapshot. Five in a row, from the archive, show whether calm is settling in or jitters are building.
  6. Context for the rules, never a signal. Harbor Now sits beside the written rules and my Operator Workstation as background. It is never a signal to trade, never a recommendation and never personal advice. The decisions belong to the person who owns the account.

Read that way, Harbor Now is good company: a clear, honest, dated picture of the day, and nothing pretending to be more.
